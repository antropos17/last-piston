# Changelog

## 0.17.0

- Build a one- to four-cylinder inline engine, choose displacement and initial wear, then start it with the key. The bank, firing cadence, mounts, oil jets and cylinder faults follow the installed build. Existing v7 saves default to the original four-cylinder engine.

## 0.16.0

- The cutaway, driving deck and diagnostics share a new workspace. The workshop header opens motor replacement and the log. Fault search matches parts, actions and symptoms.
- A snap throttle opening kicks the mounts, plays a short low exhaust beat and ejects a soot cloud. On lift-off the exhaust subsides and the cam seats are audible through run-down.

## 0.15.13

- Idle valve seats accent the moving cam over a quieter full-speed train. The starter pulls through compression, and the exhaust recording gains low end with load and manifold back pressure.
- Short workshop reflections, distinct switch contacts and a muffled tailpipe backfire give the offline recordings depth. Hot exhaust rolls into the surrounding air while steam disperses faster than oil haze.

## 0.15.12

- Pressure at opposite ends of the block alternately loads the mounts, giving the idle a rocking beat. Reduced motion attenuates the movement.
- Returning oil disturbs the sump surface. Wall films taper into dark beads, and airborne droplets catch the fixed workshop light.
- Patchy carbon coats the exhaust ports, crown edges, collector and tailpipe. Rich combustion gives the exhaust a denser soot plume.

## 0.15.11

- Faster counterweights throw a denser fan of oil from the bearing supply. Droplets leave visible streaks in flight. On the crankcase walls the oil gathers into beads, creeps downward and returns to the pan after shutdown. Wall tracks survive a v7 reload.
- At idle, short transients from the mechanical recording mark four valve closures per camshaft revolution. The ticks recede as the throttle opens.

## 0.15.10

- Cylinder pressure and reciprocating inertia rock the block on its mounts. Starter compression and braking produce distinct motion, with attenuation during reduced-motion inspection.
- Exposed cast iron and exhaust pipes carry mottled oxide and scale. Oil on the liner follows the scraper rail and film thickness, with wider wet patches where returning spray hits the crankcase.
- The healthy valve train has a quiet mechanical recording at idle. Its pitch follows shaft speed, and the idle and loaded engine recordings carry more low-frequency body.

## 0.15.9

- Each compression ring and oil-control rail wears on the side where it crosses a fragment score. Deep cuts and thin oil film accelerate wear. Worn compression faces leak under a positive chamber pressure differential into the existing vented crankcase.
- The cutaway shows the polished and recessed ring ends, a side jet down the liner and its expansion below the piston. Chamber reflections follow combustion pressure. Honed steel and machined crank webs catch the work light differently.

## 0.15.8

- A descending skirt can trap a shard against the bore. As the skirt pushes it along the wall, the shard cuts a persistent groove and takes torque from the shaft. Oil film reduces drag and scoring. Opening the gap frees the shard.
- Cylinder inspection follows the trapped shard at ×3 and shows the groove left behind after release.

## 0.15.7

- Loose chips below a piston rebound off the moving skirt. The pin recess and the shorter lip of a broken skirt leave different clearances.
- Oblique bore contacts exchange sliding speed and spin. Oil film reduces friction against the liner. Glancing strikes score the contacted wall, and the strongest hard crown strike leaves a dent that travels with that piston. Both marks survive a reload.

## 0.15.6

- Intake and exhaust flow follow piston travel and cam lift, including reverse flow after a timing fault. Open ports couple the cylinder to manifold pressure. Exhaust backpressure loads the shaft according to the working displacement.
- The cutaway shows cool intake and warm exhaust streams through the valve seats. The flame front expands from the plug across the head chamber and bore during a firing stroke.

## 0.15.5

- A trapped crankcase fragment loses material gradually and drops physical grit into the oil. The remaining body releases when the crank and bearing cap leave clearance.
- Release preserves the position and size of the worn fragment. Accumulated grit and loose chips survive a save and reload.

## 0.15.4

- High-energy impacts can break off small chips from brittle fragments. The parts pool and the oil pickup still limit and collect debris.

## 0.15.3

- Broken rod and skirt fragments detach near their sources. Pieces crossing the open liner into the sump keep their position and velocity, and crank contacts limit abrupt separation.
- A detached piston falls inside the bore before its crown separates. The movement and debris survive a save and reload.

## 0.14.27

## 0.14.25

## 0.14.24

- The cutaway shows the hot deposit, flame starting at the piston, and the growing crack. The journal and damage menu identify the fault.

## 0.14.23

## 0.14.22

## 0.14.21

- The 2D cutaway shows the tilt, scoring and actual binding point. A repeated click on the cylinder switches from a trapped fragment to the skirt when both are present. Reducing load can release the bind while the scores and bend remain. The last cylinder keeps its survival assistance.

## 0.14.20

## 0.14.19

## 0.14.18

## 0.14.17

## 0.14.16
